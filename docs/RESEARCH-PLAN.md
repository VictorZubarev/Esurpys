# Esurpys Research Plan

## 1. Purpose

This document outlines potential research directions related to Esurpys, with a focus on the usability, reliability, and usefulness of its test-based Visa Navigator.

The plan is preliminary and will evolve as the product develops and meaningful, testable research questions are identified.

This document describes possible future research. It does not claim that a formal study has been conducted or that any research findings have been established.

## 2. Research Objectives

Potential objectives include:

- Evaluating the usability and accessibility of the Visa Navigator.
- Investigating whether users understand the questions and instructions presented by the platform.
- Evaluating how users perceive the relevance and usefulness of suggested visa options and destinations.
- Identifying opportunities to improve the clarity and consistency of the test experience.
- Measuring task completion, abandonment, and navigation difficulties under clearly defined conditions.
- Identifying limitations in the information presented and the process used to explore visa options.
- Developing evidence-based improvements to the product.

Research objectives should be selected according to feasibility, available resources, and clearly defined evaluation criteria.

## 3. Preliminary Research Questions

Potential research questions include:

1. Can users understand and complete the Visa Navigator test without additional assistance?
2. Which questions or interface elements cause confusion or prevent users from completing the test?
3. How do users evaluate the clarity and perceived relevance of the visa options presented?
4. Which interface or question-design improvements produce measurable usability benefits?
5. How consistently does the test produce results when users provide comparable answers?
6. What limitations affect the usefulness, clarity, and reliability of the information presented?
7. How can the platform communicate uncertainty and encourage users to verify requirements through official sources?

These questions are preliminary. They may be refined or replaced as the product develops.

## 4. Potential Methodology

Depending on the research question, appropriate methods may include:

- Moderated or unmoderated usability testing.
- Task-completion time and completion-rate measurements.
- Structured user surveys.
- Interviews and qualitative feedback analysis.
- Comparative evaluations of alternative question wording or interface designs.
- Reproducible technical tests of the existing selection logic.
- Expert review of the clarity and presentation of visa-related information.

Before drawing conclusions, document the study design, participant selection, sample size, evaluation criteria, analysis methods, and relevant limitations.

Claims about actual visa eligibility or the accuracy of recommendations require suitable evidence and must not be inferred from user satisfaction alone.

## 5. Data Integrity, Privacy, and Ethics

Any research involving users or their data should follow appropriate ethical and privacy practices.

Principles include:

- Collect only data necessary for a clearly defined purpose.
- Obtain informed consent when appropriate or required.
- Avoid collecting sensitive personal information unless there is a justified and properly managed need.
- Protect personal and confidential information.
- Document data sources, collection methods, and analysis procedures.
- Report unfavorable results and limitations honestly.
- Avoid unsupported causal claims.
- Distinguish observed results from assumptions and interpretations.
- Follow applicable privacy and data-protection requirements.

Research involving immigration-related circumstances should take particular care to avoid exposing sensitive personal information.

## 6. Evaluation and Interpretation

Research results should be interpreted within the limits of the methods used.

For example:

- A usability study may indicate whether participants can complete the test, but does not by itself establish that the suggested visa options are legally correct.
- Positive user feedback may indicate perceived usefulness, but does not establish actual immigration eligibility.
- A small or non-representative sample may not reflect the experience of the broader user population.
- Changes in completion rates or task times should be interpreted using the study design and relevant contextual factors.

Where feasible, document reproducible procedures and preserve supporting evidence for reported conclusions.

## 7. Publication Strategy

Potential publication opportunities may include relevant academic conferences, peer-reviewed journals, or technical publications.

A submission should be considered only when the work has a sufficiently clear contribution, appropriate methodology, documented results, and meets the relevant publication standards.

Planned research is not a completed study. A submitted manuscript is not equivalent to an accepted or published paper.

No research findings, accepted papers, or publications are claimed by this plan.

## 8. Research Records

For each study, record:

- Research question and objectives.
- Study design and methodology.
- Individual contributions.
- Participant selection and sample size, where applicable.
- Data sources and collection dates.
- Evaluation criteria and analysis methods.
- Results and supporting evidence.
- Limitations and potential sources of bias.
- Reproducibility materials, where appropriate.
- Ethical and privacy considerations.
- Publication or presentation status.

Store records responsibly and avoid including confidential information in public project documentation.
