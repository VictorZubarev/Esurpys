# Esurpys Evidence Log

## 1. Purpose

This document records verifiable development milestones, individual contributions, product improvements, usage metrics, research activities, and independent recognition related to Esurpys.

Its purpose is to maintain an accurate, chronological record of the project's development and preserve supporting evidence for future professional, academic, or other formal evaluations.

This document is an evidence log, not a claim that any particular achievement, award, publication, or eligibility criterion has been established.

## 2. Project Information

- **Project:** Esurpys
- **Repository:** https://github.com/VictorZubarev/Esurpys
- **Website:** https://esurpys.vercel.app/
- **Application:** Next.js web application located in `web/`
- **Core product:** A test-based Visa Navigator that helps users explore potentially relevant visa options and destinations based on their answers.
- **Languages:** English and Russian interface content.
- **Current stage:** Active development.

## 3. Development Milestones

Record each significant milestone using verifiable information.

For every entry, include:

- **Date:** When the work was completed.
- **Milestone:** A concise description of the change or achievement.
- **Individual contribution:** What the project author or contributor personally did.
- **Related commit or release:** A Git commit hash, release tag, or relevant repository link.
- **Supporting evidence:** Screenshots, test results, deployment records, or other relevant material.
- **Verification method:** How the milestone can be independently checked.
- **Outcome:** The demonstrated result and any known limitations.

Distinguish implementation work from planned work. A commit may establish that a code change occurred, but it does not by itself demonstrate user adoption, commercial success, or independent recognition.

## 4. Product Development

Record completed features, improvements, testing activities, and deployment milestones.

Potential areas include:

- Visa Navigator questions and user experience improvements.
- Refinements to the process used to explore visa options.
- Country-specific and international test sections.
- Navigation, localization, accessibility, and responsive design.
- Reliability improvements and bug fixes.
- Build verification and deployment records.
- Documentation and development workflow improvements.

For each entry, describe what changed, why it changed, and how the result was verified.

Do not claim that a change improves recommendation accuracy unless appropriate testing or other supporting evidence demonstrates that improvement.

## 5. Usage and Product Impact

Record usage and impact when reliable evidence becomes available.

Relevant evidence may include:

- Verified user counts and active usage.
- Test completion rates.
- Return visits and user engagement.
- User feedback and documented usability evaluations.
- Referrals, partnerships, or independently verified mentions.
- Revenue or other commercial activity, where applicable.
- Measurable improvements in usability or product performance.

For each metric, record the measurement period, source, methodology, and limitations.

Distinguish website visits from unique users, registered users, and completed test sessions. Do not present estimates as verified results.

## 6. Research and Publications

If research activities are undertaken, document:

- The research question.
- The methodology and data sources.
- The author's specific contribution.
- The findings and limitations.
- The status of the work.
- Publication details and original links, if applicable.

Clearly distinguish proposed research, work in progress, submitted manuscripts, accepted papers, and published research.

Do not describe development activity as scientific research unless the work meets the relevant standards for the claim being made.

## 7. Media, Awards, and Independent Recognition

Record independently verifiable recognition, including:

- Media coverage.
- Awards and prizes.
- Invitations to judge or evaluate the work of others.
- Independent expert assessments.
- Documented partnerships.
- Other relevant third-party recognition.

For each entry, preserve the original source, date, issuing organization, selection criteria where available, and supporting documentation.

Distinguish independent recognition from self-published announcements, routine directory listings, and informal feedback.

## 8. Evidence Integrity and Recordkeeping

All entries must be accurate, attributable, and supported by appropriate evidence.

Follow these principles:

- Distinguish completed achievements from plans and intentions.
- Preserve original records and source links.
- Record dates and relevant commit hashes.
- Attribute individual contributions accurately.
- Keep evidence of the original context and outcome.
- Correct errors transparently.
- Do not invent users, metrics, awards, publications, endorsements, or partnerships.
- Do not alter evidence in a way that misrepresents the original facts.
- Avoid publishing credentials, confidential information, or sensitive personal data.

Where possible, retain original evidence in addition to summaries or screenshots.

## 9. Professional and Immigration-Related Context

Evidence may be useful in different professional, academic, or immigration-related contexts, depending on the applicable requirements.

The relevance and weight of any item must be assessed against the specific criteria and circumstances involved.

For example, repository activity may document development work, while claims about public impact or independent recognition generally require separate supporting evidence.

Neither the existence of Esurpys nor an individual project milestone automatically establishes eligibility for an immigration category, including EB-1A or EB-2 NIW.

Seek qualified legal advice when evaluating evidence for a specific immigration petition.
